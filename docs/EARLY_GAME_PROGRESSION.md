# Early-game progression

This is the intended teaching order for a new player. The design rule is one immediate need, one highlighted action, and one visible reward at a time. A system is explained when it first solves a real problem, not before.

## 1. Survival: private home base

1. **Headquarters and Cash** — The HQ is the centre of the base, controls building capacity, and opens the build menu. Cash is the everyday construction currency.
2. **Barracks** — Build inside the walls. It unlocks training and defence.
3. **Defenders** — Train five gunners and explain that trained losses must be replaced.
4. **Last Stand and Fuel** — Respond to a story-driven perimeter alarm. Teach focus targeting and introduce Fuel as the military-action resource. Explicitly label this first mission as a safe NPC training event: it costs no Fuel, cannot take land, and cannot permanently kill the new squad.
5. **Population** — Explain jobs, tax income, housing capacity, hourly food use, and hourly power use.
6. **Grain** — Build a Crop plot because settlers consume Grain every hour.
7. **Power** — Build a Solar panel because population, HQ upgrades, and expansion require Power.
8. **Collection** — Bank accumulated production and explain storage limits and offline production.

Only the resource counters needed in the current lesson are prominent during this chapter.

## 2. Expansion: local Sector

1. **Home versus Sector** — Home is the private base; Sector is the surrounding parcel map.
2. **Claiming land** — Highlight Sector and require the player to navigate there. Then open a nearby unclaimed parcel and explain ownership borders, claim cost, and the parcel's natural resource.
3. **Parcel buildings** — Explain the nine-slot limit and land suitability:
   - Housing: population capacity and tax income.
   - Farm: Grain; strongest on fertile land.
   - Oil rig: Oil; strongest on oil sands.
   - Sawmill and Mine: resource-matched Cash industry.
   - Warehouse: longer collection/storage window.
   - Solar farm and Wind turbine: Power, unlocked through Research.
4. **Extraction Run** — Tie the short mission to supplies revealed by the newly claimed land.

## 3. Growth: Headquarters and Research

1. **HQ upgrade** — More building capacity; later upgrades consume stored Power.
2. **Home building catalogue** — Explain Barracks, Crop plot, Oil pump, Solar panel, Warehouse, Research lab, Workshop, and Generator.
3. **Research** — Explain the Energy, People, War, Industry, and Trade branches; prerequisites, timers, specialization cost, HQ requirements, and late-tier Gold costs.

## 4. Trade: Market, Contracts, and Province

1. **Market resources** — Grain feeds people, Oil is a raw trade good, Fuel powers military actions, and Cash settles trades.
2. **Market controls** — Teach quick buy/sell, custom orders, best available prices, reserved goods/cash, taxes, and cancellation.
3. **Contracts** — Goods leave inventory when a truck departs and Cash arrives after delivery.
4. **Province navigation** — A Province contains multiple Sectors and shows moving shipments.
5. **Routes** — Main roads are legal and taxed; back roads introduce inspection, fines, bribes, and Caretaker standing.

## 5. Defence and repeatable action

1. **Player raids** — Distinguish them from the safe Last Stand. A player spends Fuel to attack; the server runs the defender's towers, doctrine, free garrison, and trained defenders even if that player is offline. A successful attacker can steal Cash, Oil, and Grain. A three-star result may capture one non-home parcel, but never the Headquarters or home parcel. Trained losses are persistent. Newcomer and post-defeat shields provide recovery time.
2. **Battle economy** — Fuel starts actions; Command Points refill inside the battle.
3. **Unit roles** — Gunners counter air, Launchers break armour/walls, Tanks push ground forces, Helicopters move quickly, and Strikes are limited support.
4. **Leviathan** — Introduce repeatable world-threat competition and Gold as a scarce late-game currency.

## 6. Community and government

1. **Alliance** — Shared chat, research help, defence, bank, and war score. Joining is optional.
2. **Nation and Politics** — Elections, laws, taxes, diplomacy, and war. Voting is optional when other players are unavailable.
3. **Caretaker record** — Legal/helpful actions raise standing; smuggling, bribes, and aggression lower it.

## 7. Wider world and late game

1. **Map scale** — Home → Sector → Province → Nation → Region → World.
2. **Sentinel** — A server-wide boss that uses the troops and research learned earlier.
3. **Ashlands** — Region 2 introduces richer land, war score, seizures, and persistent territorial conflict.
4. **Advanced politics** — Nation founding, coups, leadership, laws, and wars belong after the player understands the underlying economy and combat systems.

## Coverage rule for future systems

Every new feature should define:

- the player problem it solves;
- the first moment it becomes relevant;
- the exact button or world object that opens it;
- its required and rewarded resources;
- one safe guided action;
- how it connects to the next system;
- a permanent help entry after the guided step is complete.
